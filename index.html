Method. Each element's dominant visible wavelength (a flame-test or NIST persistent emission line, a documented elemental color, or — where no such data exists — a systematic placeholder) is converted to an audio pitch using audio Hz = light frequency × 10⁻¹², the scaling factor used in Walker Smith's sonified periodic table (Indiana University, ACS 2023 / SMC 2024). The raw result usually lands slightly off a true pitch — the same "microtone" effect noted in that research, shown in the Hz figures on each card — but what you actually hear when you press a key is the nearest real, equal-tempered musical note (A4 = 440 Hz), played with a warm multi-harmonic tone rather than a plain test beep.

Colors for A/B elements come from their real wavelength; the rest are placeholders and are labeled as such throughout. This is a self-contained exploratory tool, not a peer-reviewed reference.
